Gate 80 — M67: Stepper (coverage 83 → 84)

The multi-step flow control. A stepper shows an ordered sequence of
steps, marks the current one, and draws the connectors between them so
the flow's progress is visible.

Upstream splits it into a [Stepper] and a [Step], and the steps
register themselves with their parent through React context. Bonsai has
no context, so the port is data-driven: the steps are a list, and the
current step is a value the caller owns. The stepper never moves
itself; it reports a click and the caller moves it.

What the port keeps
  The indicator rules — a number until the step is complete, a tick
  once it is, a ring for the current step, a custom indicator, or no
  indicator at all. The connector fill for both the separated bar and
  the on-track segment. The compact summary row, with its Previous and
  Next controls. The measurement that decides to collapse: upstream
  compares the root's width divided by the step count against a
  minimum step width, and that decides WHAT renders, not just what is
  hidden. The status words — "completed", "warning", "error" — and the
  "Go to step N: Label" labels that carry them.

What the gates found
  The markup emitted three classes the stylesheet never styled: a
  completed step, an optional step, and the collapsed root. The first
  two now have rules. The third deliberately does NOT: a collapsed
  stepper hides its steps visually — the steps stay in the DOM for the
  accessible list, which is what the .mli promises — so hiding the
  list with display:none would take them out of the accessibility tree
  as well. The browser gate now checks that the collapsed stepper
  still has all four of its steps in the DOM.

  One assertion of ours was wrong, not the port: the collapsed
  summary was expected to carry a "Previous step" control, but the
  current step IS the first one, so there is no step to go back to and
  the control is correctly absent.

Divergences from upstream (also in the .mli)
  Upstream's [Stepper] and [Step] and the React context that joins
  them are one data-driven list here. Upstream's summary row is a
  portal target the active step writes into; there is no portal in
  Bonsai, so the stepper renders the summary from the same step data.
  Upstream's strings come from the @astryx.stepper.* and @astryx.step.*
  translation namespaces; this library has no translation layer, so
  they are spelled out in English.
