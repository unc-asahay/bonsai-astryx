Gate 99 — the DateInput was not the combobox upstream says it is

Another batch of worker reports came in, and this one was stale in the
way the others were: TimeInput, PowerSearch, SideNav, Markdown and
DateInput had all shipped long before, and four of the five workers had
produced nothing usable. But one of the reports contained a specific
claim about the *shipped* DateInput, and it was checkable, so it was
checked.

It was right, and what it found was not a style difference. The house
DateInput was not the combobox upstream defines it to be.

What upstream does

  Upstream's DateInput renders a text field and a trailing button. The
  text field is the combobox:

    role="combobox"
    aria-expanded={popover.isOpen}
    aria-haspopup="dialog"
    aria-controls={popover.isOpen ? popover.id : undefined}
    aria-autocomplete="none"
    autoComplete="off"

  and it is the field that opens the calendar: a click on the field
  opens it, ArrowDown opens it with the focus kept in the field (the
  APG's combobox pattern, forms-13), and Escape closes it. The trailing
  button opens the same calendar, for the person who would rather not
  type.

What the house port did

  The house DateInput had the text field as a plain text input, and the
  calendar opened from the trailing button only. The field's keydown
  handler knew one key: Enter, which commits. There was no ArrowDown,
  no Escape, no click, and none of the five ARIA attributes.

  So a screen reader was told nothing about the calendar: the field was
  not a combobox, did not say it opened a dialog, and did not say
  whether the calendar was open. And a keyboard user could not open
  the calendar from the field at all.

Why it shipped

  The house's own sibling had it right. The house Date_time_input —
  shipped four milestones earlier, from the same upstream family — puts
  role=combobox, aria-haspopup="dialog", aria-expanded and
  aria-autocomplete="none" on its input, opens the calendar on a click
  and on ArrowDown, and closes it on Escape. It even documents the
  contract in its own interface. DateInput was the odd one out in the
  house's own date family.

  And the browser gate had no check for any of it. It checked the
  markup, the label, the committed date and the trailing button, and it
  opened the calendar by clicking that button. It never pressed a key in
  the field, so the field's whole keyboard contract was untested — and
  so it shipped.

What was changed

  The field is now the combobox upstream describes. It carries
  role=combobox, aria-haspopup="dialog", aria-autocomplete="none" and
  autocomplete="off", and it carries aria-expanded that follows the
  calendar. A click on the field opens the calendar, ArrowDown opens
  it, Escape closes it, and Enter still commits what was typed. The
  trailing button still opens the same calendar.

  The gate now checks all of it: the five attributes on the field, the
  field's aria-expanded before and after, ArrowDown opening the
  calendar, and Escape closing it.

The one thing that is still not upstream's

  Upstream opens the calendar without taking the focus, so the person
  can carry on typing. The house's surfaces take the focus when they
  open — the house Popover does it everywhere, and so do the house
  Selector and Complex_selector — so the focus goes into the calendar
  here and comes back to the button when it closes.

  This is a house-wide model, not a DateInput mistake: the house
  Date_time_input behaves the same way. Changing it would mean changing
  the house Popover, or rebuilding the DateInput's calendar surface the
  way Complex_selector builds its own, and neither is a change worth
  making inside a date field. So it is written down in the interface
  instead of left silent — the field's own aria-expanded says which way
  the calendar is, which is what a screen reader needs.
